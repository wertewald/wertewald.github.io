WerteWald

Institute for Compassion & Environmental Awareness
Look. Discover.Choose.
Nature is a fundamental point of reference for WerteWald. It is not only about experiencing nature, but also about the awareness of being part of a larger, living system.

What changes when perception is not only the beginning of learning, but also a way of gaining access to oneself?

What if an insight changes the way we look at our own lives — and with it, the way we perceive connections?

Could our view be influenced by different “lenses”? Could experiences become filters that shape our perception — and perhaps sometimes distort it?

These and other questions are explored by WerteWald on different levels, encouraging renewed attention to oneself, to others and to the environment.

People are invited to ask questions, shift perspectives and consciously examine their own conclusions — with the aim of gaining orientation and acting with greater self-determination.

The aim is not to provide ready-made answers. WerteWald creates spaces in which people can discover for themselves what they perceive, what they understand, what resonates with them and what possibilities may emerge from this.

WerteWald offers impulses for more conscious perception and reflection with and within nature, and for discovering new perspectives — for oneself, in interaction with others and in respectful relationship with nature.

Observation can lead to insight. Insight can lead to relationship. Relationship can lead to connectedness. And where connectedness grows, responsibility and possibilities for action can become visible.

WerteWald Educational Approach

WerteWald is an educational approach centred on perception, inquiry and perspective-taking. Wherever possible, it begins with concrete experiences and observations. These are connected with reflection and subject knowledge, making different perspectives and wider connections visible while creating space for people to develop their own sense of orientation and recognise different possibilities.

Questions are not used simply to lead towards predetermined answers. Openness, not knowing and different well-founded conclusions are allowed to remain part of the learning process.

The WerteWald Circle

The WerteWald Circle shows how conscious perception can set a process of development in motion.

Perception
→ Insight
↔ Relationship
↔ Connectedness
→ Responsibility
→ Possibilities for Action
→ Sharing / Protecting
What matters is the movement: Perception can open up orientation, and orientation can open up room for action.

This dynamic process can support people in dealing more consciously with change, uncertainty and different perspectives, in recognising their own possibilities and in shaping their lives with greater self-determination.

It can strengthen self-efficacy, encourage conscious action and support the ability to find orientation in complex situations. Consciously engaging with one’s own reactions, needs and possibilities for action can also help people deal more resiliently with change and strain.

Through shifts in perspective, conscious communication and collective action, social skills and the capacity for relationships can continue to develop — in relation to oneself, to other people and to the environment.

The Seven Pillars of WerteWald

1

Mindfulness

For WerteWald, mindfulness means consciously paying attention to the present moment — to oneself, to other people and to the environment.

It begins with the senses, but goes beyond them. Thoughts, feelings, reactions and impressions from the world around us are noticed without immediately interpreting or judging them.

2

Perception & Reflection

WerteWald begins with conscious perception. Observations, personal reactions and experiences are consciously considered and reflected upon. A distinction is made between observation, knowledge, assumption and personal interpretation.

3

Nature & Science

Nature is not only a place of learning within WerteWald, but also a starting point for questions, insights and development. Scientific background can help place observations in context and provide orientation, without fully replacing personal experiences or open questions.

4

Appreciation

For WerteWald, appreciation begins when something is experienced not simply as existing, but as valuable. It combines respect with gratitude and an awareness that what is valuable should not be taken for granted. From this can grow a desire to treat it with care, to preserve it and to protect it.

Connectedness and appreciation influence one another. Experiencing connectedness can deepen the recognition of value. And when something is truly appreciated, the way we relate to it changes.
5

Connectedness, Relationship & Compassion

For WerteWald, connectedness means experiencing oneself not as isolated, but as part of a larger context.

Relationship and connectedness interact with one another. Insights can open up a more conscious relationship with oneself, with other people and with nature. At the same time, the experience of connectedness can make relationships more visible, more conscious and more deeply felt.

Where people experience themselves as part of a larger context, compassion can grow, along with an awareness that their own actions have an impact.

6

Perspective Shift & Orientation

Different perspectives can exist side by side. WerteWald invites people to shift perspectives, to tolerate uncertainty and to examine their own point of view where it makes sense in the given situation. Orientation does not come from ready-made answers, but from consciously placing things in context and seeking to understand them.

7

Freedom of Choice

WerteWald aims to strengthen people in recognising their own possibilities, making conscious decisions and becoming aware of their room for action.

Individual freedom of choice remains a central point of reference. WerteWald assumes that this freedom is fundamentally present, but that it can be obscured by experiences, expectations, judgements or external circumstances and may at times be barely noticeable.

Educational Formats

Nature is a fundamental point of reference for WerteWald. Depending on the format, it can serve as a space for direct experience, a learning partner, a living presence or a connecting element for different topics and questions.

The individual formats address different age groups, stages of life, topics and approaches, and are designed to suit the respective target group, situation and learning environment.

In this way, a connected and dynamic educational system emerges, linking different age groups, stages of life and approaches.

WerteWald Roots

For children aged approximately 6–10.

WerteWald Roots invites children to experience nature directly, observe for themselves, ask questions and make their own discoveries.

Nature is understood not only as a place of learning, but as a learning partner. Movement, sensory experiences, exploration, shared experiences and reflection work together.

Children are encouraged to discover things for themselves wherever possible before answers are provided. Community comes before competition, diversity is a natural part of learning together, and connectedness with nature creates space for appreciation, compassion and environmental awareness.

Status: Practically tested in 2026 — further modules in development.

WerteWald Plus

For older children and young people aged approximately 10–18.

WerteWald Plus accompanies young people through a stage of life shaped by physical changes, growing independence, new social experiences and an increasing engagement with themselves and society.

The focus is on personal skills, self-reflection and orientation. Young people are invited to become more aware of their bodies, emotions, thinking, relationships and social influences, and to place them in context.

Nature serves as a shared language and a source of orientation. Metaphors from nature can help make complex topics easier to understand, reveal connections and support the development of personal insights.

Status: In format and module development.

WerteWald Maps

For parents and other caregivers.

WerteWald Maps offers orientation for adults who accompany children and young people.

The format invites them to look at situations from different perspectives and to become more aware of their own role in their interactions with others.

Behaviour, needs, challenges and possible connections are not judged too quickly, but considered in a differentiated way. Shifts in perspective and reflection can help people place situations in context and discover new possibilities in how they relate to one another.

Status: In format and module development.

WerteWald Communication

For young people and adults.

WerteWald Communication focuses on language and its impact.

At its heart is the question of how communication influences perception, relationships and mutual understanding — and, conversely, how experiences, expectations, judgements and personal patterns of interpretation shape our inner dialogue and our communication with others.

WerteWald aims to strengthen people in using language more consciously — in conversations with others as well as in the way they communicate with themselves.

Status: In format and module development.

WerteWald Collective

For young people and adults.

WerteWald Collective looks at what shapes us long before we consciously think about it: social ideas, cultural patterns, role models, judgements and expectations.

But how much of this actually belongs to one’s own personality? How much individuality flows into our behaviour and everyday decisions — and how much follows patterns we have adopted without consciously choosing them?

These and other questions are explored in WerteWald Collective from different perspectives.

Status: In format and module development.

WerteWald Sense

For adults, families and groups.

WerteWald Sense is a body-oriented format that uses the available senses and ways of perceiving to bring people more strongly back into direct experience and into awareness of their own body.

The aim is not to assume the presence of particular senses, but to consciously use the ways of perceiving that are available to each individual.

In this way, WerteWald Sense aims to strengthen self-perception and support people in experiencing their bodies more consciously as a source of information and orientation.

Status: Conceptual idea.

WerteWald Ventures

For young people and adults.

WerteWald Ventures combines experiences in nature with adventure, play and community.

Puzzles, roles, stories and shared challenges create approaches in which nature is experienced not only as a place of retreat, but as something we encounter as living — a space for excitement, enjoyment and shared experience.

Status: Conceptual idea.

WerteWald Pulse & Move

For young people and adults.

WerteWald Pulse & Move combines movement in nature with body awareness, rhythm, sound and supportive verbal prompts.

Conscious movement, direct contact with natural ground and different acoustic stimuli create a space for direct experience in which people can feel their bodies more intensely, discover them in new ways and develop enjoyment of movement.

The focus is on appreciation for one’s own body — not as something to be taken for granted, but as a living and multifaceted system.

Status: Conceptual idea.

WerteWald Digitals

For young people and adults.

WerteWald Digitals focuses on orientation within digital information environments.

WerteWald aims to strengthen people in placing information in context, examining sources and forms of presentation, and becoming more aware of how language, images, selection, technical systems and the way content is framed and presented can influence perception.

Status: Conceptual idea.

WerteWald Seniors

For older people.

A conceptual educational approach aimed at strengthening independence, digital participation and orientation in dealing with technological change.

The aim is to support older people in understanding digital requirements more clearly and managing them as independently as possible.

Status: Conceptual idea.

WerteWald 18+

Not all topics belong in formats for children or families.

WerteWald 18+ creates space for content aimed specifically at adults and addresses social, personal and interpersonal topics that are direct, uncomfortable or sensitive.

It openly explores questions around boundaries, self-determination, agency, power, manipulation, sexualisation and other forms of influence and boundary-crossing behaviour.

WerteWald Puppets

For adults.

WerteWald Puppets focuses on situations in which personal agency is restricted, undermined or threatened.

The format considers manipulative behaviour, boundary violations, dynamics of power and dependency, and the ways in which perception, decisions and freedom of action can be influenced.

The format explores how such processes can be recognised, what effects they may have, and what possibilities exist for preserving or regaining personal agency.

Status: Conceptual idea.

WerteWald Abroad

You are here on holiday. But someone else lives here.

Tourism brings together visitors, places, nature and people. What means relaxation and freedom to some is, for others, the place where they live, work and go about their everyday lives.

WerteWald Abroad focuses on this relationship and on the traces a stay can leave behind.

It looks at nature and resources, local structures, quality of life, encounters between visitors and local people, and the many small decisions whose effects often only become visible when considered together.

What do you receive from this place – and what do you give back to it?

WerteWald develops practical and interactive approaches that invite people to observe, explore and see things from different perspectives.

Visitors are not simply encouraged to follow rules, but to understand the wider connections and decide for themselves how they want to engage with a place, its people and its natural environment.

Status: In conceptual development.

WerteWald Fauna

Observe animals. Question relationships. Discover responsibility.

WerteWald Fauna invites us to see animals as living beings in their own right – with their own abilities, needs, perceptions, relationships and ways of life.

How do we encounter other living beings? What role do we give ourselves? Where do we protect – and where do we control, use or restrict?

The question is not only what we know about animals, but also:

What might we not know?

And:

What responsibility arises from the fact that humans can make decisions about the lives of other living beings?

WerteWald Fauna is based on the understanding that animals are valuable.

Pilot Phase & Development

Where does WerteWald stand today?

WerteWald was established in March 2026 and has been in its development and pilot phase ever since.

With WerteWald Roots, the first modules were tested in practice with groups of children in August 2026 and systematically evaluated.

The experience gained through this practical testing continues to inform the further development of the format and the broader educational system.

What are the next steps in development?

In the next phase of development, WerteWald Roots will continue to be tested in practice and further developed for more regular use in practice. Options for regular implementation are currently being explored.

Building on the experience gained with WerteWald Roots, WerteWald Maps is being further developed as a complementary format for parents and other caregivers.

At the same time, the format and module development of WerteWald Communication is continuing. Suitable target groups, learning environments and ways of testing the format in practice in the future will be defined more precisely as the development process progresses.

How are the formats developed?

The educational formats of WerteWald are developed step by step: from conception through practical testing and evaluation to further development and reapplication in practice.

Throughout this process, content, methods, conditions and practical framework are continuously considered in relation to how well they fit the respective target group, learning environment and nature of the format.

What future directions are emerging?

For WerteWald Roots, possibilities for more regular practical implementation and further collaboration are currently being explored together with a local municipality.

At the same time, the educational system is gradually being expanded to include additional formats. The initial focus is on development steps that build meaningfully on existing experience and target groups.

In the long term, the different WerteWald formats are intended to function independently while remaining connected — as different approaches to perception, relationship, orientation and shaping one’s life with greater self-determination.

Pilot Modules 2026

Water Researchers –
Every Drop Counts

For children aged 6–10.

Water Researchers – Every Drop Counts was delivered as a three-hour module as part of a municipality’s holiday programme.

The children explored why people, animals and plants need water and how everything is interconnected. Working in small research teams, they investigated different water-related clues, made observations at the biotope and deepened their understanding through hands-on activities — including building an insect drinking station.

Nature Guardians –
Play, Fun & Responsibility

For children aged 6–10.

Nature Guardians – Play, Fun & Responsibility was delivered as a three-hour module as part of a municipality’s holiday programme.

The children investigated traces of human impact on nature and explored potential dangers to animals and habitats. Through search, sorting and rescue activities, responsibility was not only discussed, but made tangible through practical experience.

Cooperation

WerteWald is open to cooperation with municipalities, educational institutions, nature and environmental stakeholders, as well as other partners whose fields of work and objectives align with the project’s core ideas.

WerteWald sees openness as a strength. The shared framework should provide enough orientation without unnecessarily limiting creative possibilities.

Collaboration may also open up new paths. Ideas, impulses and unconventional approaches are welcome when they contribute meaningfully to the further development of WerteWald.

Cooperation should develop wherever there is a suitable setting for WerteWald and a viable basis for working together.

Behind WerteWald

WerteWald was developed by Andrea Marija Zivkovic.

Personal experiences, motherhood, a strong connection to nature, and many years of engagement with perception, communication, social influences and acting with greater self-determination shape the development of the project.

This is complemented by training in business and the natural sciences, leadership experience, analytical work in laboratory settings and diagnostics, as well as entrepreneurial experience.

Further professional training in nature and environmental education, methodology and didactics, inclusion, as well as prevention and resilience education, is part of this ongoing development.

WerteWald understands development as an ongoing process. Continuing to learn, reflect, take in new insights and keep evolving are therefore a natural part of it.

Further development means keeping moving — like a forest that changes, grows and yet retains its essence.
